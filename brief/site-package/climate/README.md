# Climate

The organiser **does not supply** a climate series. Below: what the climate means for design, where to get data, and how to declare it. Write the dataset, years and station or grid cell you used into `assumptions.json` and `sources.json`. **A gap in the data the organiser supplied is never a reason to mark an entry down.**

## Conditions a scheme must answer

| Condition | What it means for design |
|---|---|
| Arid | Rain is scarce and evaporation far exceeds it; every planted area and water surface enters the water balance. |
| Strong sun | The basis of the solar resource — and shade decides whether people can go outside. |
| Large daily range | Very hot summer days, near-freezing winter nights; thermal mass and night ventilation are available tools. |
| The Zonda | A dry, hot, strong, dust-laden downslope wind from the Andes to the west. It affects panel soiling and cleaning water, air-cooled intakes, outdoor comfort and street orientation. State the scheme's stance: block, channel, or step aside. |
| Storms and flash floods | Channels are dry most of the year; an upstream storm can send a flood down them within a short time. No permanent construction on a dry channel. |
| Dark sky | San Juan province hosts astronomical observatories. Give a night-lighting and light-pollution strategy; distance to the observatories and whether a regulation applies are unverified by the organiser — declare your assumption. |

## Where to get data

| Use | Dataset |
|---|---|
| Solar radiation and PV yield | PVGIS (EU Joint Research Centre): https://joint-research-centre.ec.europa.eu/photovoltaic-geographical-information-system-pvgis_en |
| Hourly or daily meteorology, global grid | NASA POWER (Data Access Viewer): https://power.larc.nasa.gov/docs/tutorials/data-access-viewer/quick-start/ |
| Station observations | SMN Argentina, San Juan Aero station |

The site centre is about 30.95° S, 68.05° W.
